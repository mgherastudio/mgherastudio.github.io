Privacy Policy

Mtsvadi Catcher, a mobile game by Mghera. In effect from 23 November 2026.

This policy explains what happens to your data when you play Mtsvadi Catcher on Android or iPhone. "We" means Mghera, the developer of the game.

  • The game has no accounts, and it does not ask for your name, email address, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or precise location.
  • We do not run a server for the game. Your scores and settings are not sent to us.
  • The game shows ads from Google AdMob. Google collects some data from your device to do this.
  • There are no purchases in the game.

Data saved by the game

The game saves these on your device:

  • your best score, total points and unlocked pieces
  • your sound, music, volume and vibration settings
  • ad timing: when the game was first opened, when an ad was last shown, and how many runs have been played since the last ad or remain ad-free
  • on iPhone, a note that the tracking question has been handled

We cannot see this data. If device backup is switched on, your phone may copy it to your own Google or Apple account and restore it when you reinstall the game.

Ads and the data Google collects

The game includes the Google Mobile Ads SDK (AdMob) and Google's consent tool, the User Messaging Platform. To provide ads, measure and analyse them, and prevent fraud, Google may collect and share:

  • your IP address, which can be used to estimate your general location
  • device and account identifiers: on Android, the advertising ID (a code your phone gives apps for ads), the app set ID and, where they apply, identifiers linked to accounts signed in on the device; on iPhone, the advertising identifier or other identifiers limited to this app or developer
  • how you interact with the app and its ads, such as app launches, taps and video views
  • diagnostic and performance data, such as how fast the app starts, how often it freezes, how much energy it uses, and crash logs
  • advertising data, such as which ads you have seen

Google's ad and consent software also saves and reads its own data on your device. Examples are your consent choice, identifiers used for ads, and cookies or similar files used by the ads. Ad companies working with Google may do the same. Where the law requires your consent for this, the consent form asks for it.

This data goes to Google, and may be shared with other companies that take part in showing ads. Google's own rules apply to it:

  • How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services
  • Google Privacy Policy

Every company that receives your data through the game must protect it at least as well as this policy describes and the app stores require.

We receive ad reports from Google, such as the number of ads shown and the money earned. We do not use them to identify players.

Your choices about ads

  • Consent form. Where it is required, including the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's form asks for your choice before the game loads any ads. On Android it usually appears when the game opens. On iPhone it appears after a finished run.
  • If you say no. The game plays the same and may still show ads. Those ads are not personalised. Google still receives the data needed to show them, such as your IP address.
  • Before you choose. Each time the game opens, Google's consent tool contacts Google to check whether a form is needed. This sends Google your IP address and some technical data before you have made a choice.
  • Where no form is needed. The game connects to Google's ad service when it opens. On Android it starts loading ads then. On iPhone no ads load until after your first finished run. Google may use your advertising ID to personalise ads. On iPhone, the advertising identifier is available only if you allow tracking.
  • Changing your mind. On the start screen, tap Privacy choices to change or withdraw your choice. It needs an internet connection. If the button is missing, no form applies where you are, or the game could not reach Google when it opened.
  • iPhone tracking. After your first finished run, the game may show Apple's tracking question. The game plays the same if you say no. You can change your answer in the iPhone's Settings, under Privacy & Security (Privacy on older iPhones), Tracking.
  • Advertising ID. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's privacy settings.

When you email us

If you write to us, we receive your email address and whatever you include in the message. We use it only to reply and solve the problem. Our mailbox is provided by Google (Gmail). We keep messages only as long as needed for that, and delete them sooner if you ask.

Legal basis

Saving your scores and settings on your device is needed for the game to work.

For personalised ads, and wherever the law requires consent for ads or for saving ad data on your device, we rely on the choice you make in the consent form. You do not have to agree, and the game plays the same if you do not. You have the right to withdraw your consent at any time; that does not change what was done before.

Where consent is not required, for ads that are not personalised, for counting ads and for stopping fraud, we rely on our legitimate interest in paying for a free game with ads.

We handle emails on the basis of our legitimate interest in answering you.

Keeping and deleting data

  • On your device. Game data stays until you clear the app's data or uninstall the game. Copies in your phone's backup are managed in your Google or Apple account.
  • Held by Google. Google keeps ad data under its own policies. We hold no copy we could look up or delete for you, so please use the choices above and Google's privacy controls.
  • Emails. Ask us and we will delete them.

Security

Game data is kept in the app's private storage on your device. Google states that data collected by its ads SDK on Android is encrypted while being sent. No method of storing or sending data is completely secure.

Where data is processed

Google may process data in countries other than yours, including the United States. Google explains the protections it uses on its data transfer frameworks page.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to see, correct, delete or restrict the use of your personal data, to object to its use, to receive a copy, or to withdraw consent.

  • Emails you sent us. Write to us and we will act on your request.
  • Game data. It is on your device, and in your phone's backup if you use one, so you control it there.
  • Ad data. Google holds it, not us. Use Privacy choices in the game, where shown, and Google's own controls. If you write to us, we will help you find the right place.

You can also complain to the data protection authority (the privacy regulator) in your country.

Children and teenagers

Mtsvadi Catcher is meant for players aged 13 and over and is not directed at younger children.

The game does not ask your age. In some countries you must be older than 13 (up to 16) to agree to personalised ads by yourself. If you are younger than that, ask a parent or guardian to answer the consent form with you, or choose not to consent. The game plays the same.

If you are a parent and believe your child's data was collected through the game, email us. We will delete anything we hold and help you find where to ask Google.
